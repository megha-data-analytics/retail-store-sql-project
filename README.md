# Retail Store Database — SQL Mini Project

## 📌 Project Overview

The Retail Store Database project demonstrates the use of SQL to manage and analyze retail business data. It covers customers, products, orders, payments, and product reviews.

The project focuses on retrieving information, analyzing sales and customer activity, and answering business questions using SQL queries.

## 🎯 Project Objectives

- Explore retail customer and product data.
- Retrieve and filter records using SQL.
- Analyze orders, sales amounts, and payments.
- Identify customer purchasing activity.
- Join multiple tables to combine business information.
- Use subqueries and set operations to solve analytical problems.

## 🛠️ Tools & Technologies

- **SQL**
- **MySQL** (for the MySQL-compatible queries)
- **GitHub** (project documentation and version control)

## 🗂️ Database Tables

The project works with the following tables:

- `customers`
- `products`
- `orders`
- `order_items`
- `payments`
- `product_review` / `product_reviews` — confirm the actual table name in your database.

## 📚 SQL Concepts Covered

### Level 1 — SQL Basics
- SELECT statements
- DISTINCT
- WHERE conditions
- BETWEEN and IN
- LIKE
- ORDER BY

### Level 2 — Filtering and Formatting
- Column aliases
- LEFT JOIN for data-quality checks
- Calculated columns
- CONCAT and COALESCE
- Date extraction
- Stock filtering

### Level 3 — Aggregations
- COUNT, SUM, and AVG
- GROUP BY and HAVING
- Customer order analysis
- Sales by customer
- Product sales by category
- Daily order analysis
- Payment method analysis

### Level 4 — Multi-Table Queries
- INNER JOIN
- LEFT JOIN
- RIGHT JOIN
- Combining customer, order, and payment information

### Level 5 — Subqueries
- Above-average product prices
- Customers who have placed orders
- Orders above customer-specific averages
- Customers who have never ordered
- Products that have never been ordered
- Highest-value orders per customer

### Level 6 — Set Operations
- UNION
- Combining customer activity from orders and reviews
- Identifying customers who have both ordered and reviewed products

## 📊 Business Applications

The queries can help a retail business investigate:

- Customer purchasing activity
- Customer sales contribution
- Product and category performance
- Payment method usage
- Products with zero stock
- Customers who have not placed orders
- Products that have never been ordered
- High-value customer orders
